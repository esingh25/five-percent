the long way

the loop, traced from twenty-three years of location data and blurred until the streets stopped being real streets

I started walking at night because I could not sleep. The sleeping sorted itself out around 2031 and I kept walking.

The loop is four streets. Out past the elementary school, left at the church with the broken sign, down the hill, back up the other side. Fifty-one minutes. I have walked it in rain, in a walking boot, and at two in the morning in January because the apartment was too quiet to stay in.

The phone counts the steps and gives me a number, and the number is enormous now. It counts the night I went around eleven times the same as the night I went around once.

I have never walked it in the other direction. I thought about it tonight, standing at the corner, and then I went the way I go.
